How fast the price climbs

Who sees our ads

The words we buy decide what someone is searching for. These settings decide who sees the ad. We pay more to reach the people most likely to become students, and less to reach everyone else.

How the pricing works. Google reports a range for every search, the cheapest and the most that advertisers have paid. Winning a small slice of a search is cheap. Winning most of it means outbidding everyone else, so the price climbs toward the top of that range. The planner moves the price as you move the share, and the three buttons in the cart change how steeply it climbs.

How we bid. Every number below adjusts what we pay for a click. Google applies these only when we set our own bids, so we start there. Once we have enough form fills, we can hand bidding to Google and these settings become reporting rather than controls.

Age brackets. Google sets them and we bid on each one separately. A search campaign cannot filter on whether someone has children, so we go by age and let the two parent brackets carry the premium. Parents of teenagers are handled in the next box.

Income. Google does not know anyone's income. It sorts ZIP codes by average income and estimates from there. Our top states are already wealthy ZIP codes, so we keep this adjustment small to avoid paying the premium twice.

Unknown age. Google puts people it cannot identify into an unknown age group, and most high school students land there. We keep showing ads to that group.

3. Groups we add on top

Google can tell us things about people beyond what they typed. We use five of those.

  • People Google knows are shopping for a college right now
  • Parents of teenagers, age 13 to 17
  • People studying for the SAT or ACT
  • People who compare prices before they buy, which fits our cost and scholarship ads
  • People who already visited our site and did not finish the form

The first month. We watch which of these groups responds without blocking anyone else from seeing the ad. After a month we raise the bid on the groups that are working.

Competitor interest. Google does not offer browsing-based audiences on a search campaign, so we buy the comparison searches as keywords instead, such as "elon vs high point university."

Site visitors. Google requires 1,000 people on the list before it can be used on search. Collection starts the day the tracking tag goes live.

4. When they see it

Families look at colleges together, and that happens at night and on weekends.

Students look on a phone and parents fill out the form on a laptop, so laptops are worth more to us than the click count suggests.

Time of year. More than half our class applies Early Decision in the fall. We spend the most from September to November, and again in March and April to reach students who have already been admitted.
